# Triage Guidelines

**Owner:** Student Services
**Last revised:** Academic year 2025-26

How to route an incoming student ticket. Read this before the department
policies — it tells you *how* to decide, they tell you *what* the rules are.

## Establish who is asking

If the ticket names a roll number, look the student up before deciding anything.
Their actual fee, hostel and examination status usually settles the question far
faster than reading the message carefully does.

If no roll number is given and the message needs one to resolve, say so in the
suggested action rather than guessing. **Never invent a roll number.**

## Find the real department

Route on the *underlying* problem, not the surface symptom. Students describe
where they noticed a problem, not where it lives.

| The student says | The ticket belongs to |
|---|---|
| "the portal says my fees are pending" | accounts — the portal is only a view |
| "I can't download my hall ticket" | examinations, but check for a fee block first |
| "I can't log in at all" | it_support |
| "my scholarship hasn't been applied" | accounts |
| "I need a bonafide for my passport" | admissions |
| "my room hasn't been allotted" | hostel |
| "I want my answer script rechecked" | examinations |

A ticket can touch two departments. Route it to the one that has to act *first* —
a fee-blocked student who can't get a hall ticket needs Accounts, not
Examinations, because Examinations can do nothing until the block clears.

## Set urgency honestly

Urgency is about consequences, not about tone. A student writing in capitals is
not automatically urgent, and a politely worded message about a visa appointment
tomorrow certainly is.

- **critical** — a deadline is being missed *right now*. An examination starts
  tomorrow, a visa interview is this week, a scholarship application closes today.
- **high** — the student is blocked and it will become critical within days.
- **normal** — a real problem with time to resolve it.
- **low** — a question, a request for information, no deadline.

Most tickets are normal. If you find yourself marking everything high, you have
made the field useless.

## When to hand it to a human

Set `needs_human` to true, every time, for:

- **Money movement** — refunds, waivers, disputed payments, fines.
- **Policy exceptions** — anything asking to bend a rule or a deadline.
- **Discipline, safety or wellbeing** — no exceptions, whatever the tone.
- **Third-party requests** — anyone asking about a student who isn't them,
  including parents.
- **Original documents** — any physical release of certificates.
- **Your own uncertainty.** If you are not confident, that *is* the signal.

The cost of escalating unnecessarily is a few minutes of someone's time. The
cost of not escalating is a student harmed by a wrong answer. These are not
equivalent, so do not treat them as a balanced trade-off.

## Write a useful summary

The summary is read by a human who has thirty seconds and forty tickets. Give
them the facts, not the feelings: what is blocked, since when, and what has been
tried. Twenty-five words.
